# Problem Statement

## Client
EM Services Pte Ltd (Town Council managing agent)

## Background
Estate biodiversity management currently operates reactively. Complaints about
community cats, pigeons, rodents, and flora health arrive through scattered
channels — phone calls, emails, paper forms — and are handled in isolation.
Each complaint triggers a separate response, often a contractor call-out, with
no consolidation across similar issues at the same location. There is no
unified view of estate health, no mechanism to detect recurring patterns, and
no way to measure whether interventions actually reduce repeat incidents.

## Core Problem
Town Council managers cannot see across the estate at a glance, cannot detect
emerging hotspots before they escalate, and cannot tell whether money spent on
interventions is reducing risk or just deferring it. Field officers file
observations on paper or in disconnected systems, making pattern detection
impossible. Every complaint is treated as isolated, even when five residents
report the same block in the same week.

## Compounding Factors
- Rodent management in particular is reactive: complaints trigger treatment,
  treatment provides temporary relief, the issue recurs. The cycle repeats
  because the underlying cause (food waste from feeding activity, structural
  gaps, poor bin centre hygiene) is never addressed systematically.
- Contractor call-outs are expensive. Every call-out that could have been
  consolidated or prevented through early detection is wasted spend.
- Staff have no tool to prioritise. Without a ranked view of which blocks need
  attention most urgently, time and resources are allocated by whoever complained
  loudest, not by actual risk.

## Proposed Solution
A unified biodiversity intelligence platform that consolidates flora, fauna and
rodent observations into a single operational view. The system surfaces
patterns a human reviewer would miss, recommends actions, and routes them
through a human approval step before any money is committed — satisfying the
client's stated constraint that every contractor call-out must have a person
in the loop.

Key capabilities:
- Estate risk index giving managers a single glance at overall estate health
- Hotspot detection across fauna sightings and rodent reports
- AI-assisted rodent risk assessment with recurrence awareness
- Work order pipeline tracking the full lifecycle from complaint to resolution
- Prevention scorecard measuring whether interventions reduce repeat incidents
- Simulated sensor integration layer showing what real-time monitoring would
  add once the RATSENSE pilot data feeds in

## Success Criteria
- A field officer can file a rodent observation and receive an AI assessment
  that factors in prior reports at the same location
- A manager can see which blocks are highest risk and approve a consolidated
  work order covering multiple related complaints in one action
- The system can demonstrate a measurable reduction in repeat rodent reports
  at blocks where work orders have been completed
- All actions that commit resources require explicit human approval
